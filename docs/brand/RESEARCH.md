## Why amber

Amber isn't a style choice here. It is already what a plow means on the road.

- A UK code of practice, [as quoted on a trade forum](https://arbtalk.co.uk/forums/topic/61154-chapter-8-markings), requires a flashing amber beacon on vehicles clearing snow or gritting, and a [Maine bill](https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/LDs/115/115-LD-0607.pdf) specifies amber lights facing front and rear. [Iowa](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/321.393.pdf) and [Utah](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R920-8-5) start from amber and allow additions.
- Chevron markings are [common on work vehicles and set locally](https://g1-graphics-site.pages.dev/knowledge/municipal-fleet/public-works-striping.html). There is no single US standard, and real ones are often [red and fluorescent yellow](https://arbtalk.co.uk/forums/topic/61154-chapter-8-markings). So Flake's stripe is a reference, and must never pass for a real safety marking.

## Why a dark driver screen

- A [vision-science study of displays in the dark](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk09dcnliv.pdf) finds long-wavelength light, amber to red, is seen through daylight vision while barely affecting night vision, and recommends keeping such displays dim.
- [Pilot guidance](https://www.caa.govt.nz/assets/publications/gaps/night-vfr.pdf) warns that large bright screens erode dark adaptation, and [night modes exist for this reason](https://www.flyingmag.com/cockpit-lighting-choices/).
- I found no truck-specific contrast or text-size standard. The 7:1 floor and 64px targets are Flake's own choices, not a cited requirement. Test them on a real phone in a real cab.

## Why this type

[Barlow](https://github.com/jpt/Barlow) was drawn by Jeremy Tribby with Clearview, Highway Gothic, DIN, street signs and license plates as influences. It is free under the SIL Open Font License, so it can go on the app, trucks and print at no cost. Claims about its long-distance legibility come from a specimen page and aren't independently verified.

## Risks in the name

**"Flake" means unreliable.** Dictionaries define it as [someone who proves unreliable or fails to follow through](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/flake), and in the US it can also mean [someone eccentric](https://daily.wordreference.com/2025/07/24/intermediate-word-of-the-day-flake/). For a business whose whole promise is showing up, that is a real risk and also a memorable joke. Decide this deliberately before spending on vehicles or print.

**Trademark.** Snowflake Inc. has [opposed similar marks](https://www.docketalarm.com/trademarks/90280784/SNOWFALL/) such as SNOWFALL, and was itself [sued by Yeti Data](https://www.theregister.com/2020/08/06/yeti_snowflake_lawsuit/) over the name. I didn't find or rule out an existing mark for "Flake", because I didn't run a USPTO search. Have a trademark attorney clear it before paying for print, vehicle wraps or a filing. This isn't legal advice.

## Practical notes

A snow contractor's [logo brief](https://www.designcrowd.com/jobs/job/1180438) asked for a logo that shows up well on white trucks. That is one operator's request, but it is the reason for the one-color truck rule. [Lucide](https://lucide.dev) is the icon set because it is free and already in the product.
